Explainable AI Artifact Explanation

Arvinnd Chowdary Kuchipudi
Model Development · AIML-501
Professor Vincent Sichula

Explainable AI helps people understand and scrutinize model behavior, while validation establishes how reliably a system performs for a defined use. This graphic organizes the subject into four connected categories: Explainable AI and its importance, challenges in explainability, validation and performance metrics, and current solutions and techniques. A worked policy-assistant example connects the sections. The challenges explain why GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Llama outputs can be difficult to interpret. The industry section distinguishes research into internal features and circuits from model documentation and behavioral safety testing. These categories were chosen according to the evidence each provides: candidate mechanisms, disclosed context, or observed performance. Sorting by evidence avoids implying that documentation or test scores establish how an individual answer was computed. OpenAI and Anthropic illustrate mechanistic research; Google illustrates both Gemma interpretability tools and separate Gemini transparency efforts; Meta illustrates disclosure and application testing. This separation avoids treating open weights, citations, or fluent reasoning as proof of a faithful explanation (Anthropic, 2025; Google, n.d.; Google DeepMind, 2024; Meta, 2024; OpenAI, 2024).

The numbered layout guides a non-technical reader from the problem to available evidence and then to a practical decision. Consistent colors and short labels separate challenges from approaches and evaluation checks, while the same policy-assistant example connects correctness, groundedness, and robustness without requiring knowledge of neural-network mathematics. Hypothetical correctness, groundedness, robustness, and subgroup calculations make the metrics concrete; these are not measured results or comparisons between providers. The regulatory and audit panel highlights documentation, privacy, and access tradeoffs (NIST, 2023). Correctness, subgroup results, robustness, and explanation faithfulness answer different questions, so the design does not combine them into a universal trust score. A relationship diagram uses converging arrows to show how explanation methods, validation evidence, and documented limitations jointly support informed human review. A text version, responsive layout, and scalable vector download support accessibility and reuse. Together, understandable explanations, representative held-out testing, human review, and documented limits support accountability and informed reliance; none alone guarantees safe or accurate behavior (Google Cloud, n.d.; Meta, 2024; Phillips et al., 2021).
